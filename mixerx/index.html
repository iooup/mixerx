01 / THE 2-IN-1

The mixer and the visuals, one session. 🎭

One session, one clock, one set of controls — not a player with a screensaver bolted on. Here's what each face does.

🎛️ FACE 01 — THE MIXER

A real audio engine, not a player.

Sample-accurate transport on AudioWorklets. Beat grids, keys and song sections are analysed on your machine, so every blend lands on the phrase.

  • 3-band EQ with kills, filter, CUE, limiter
  • Hot cues, loops, beat jump, pads, nudge
  • Filters that find the next track: key, tempo ±6 %, energy
  • LEARN lessons, and Web MIDI with soft takeover

🪩 FACE 02 — THE STAGE

Visuals that follow the track.

A WebGPU stage that reads the music and your hands: the drop is telegraphed four bars ahead, a LOW kill drops the floor, a filter sweep blurs the scene.

  • 16 looks: 8 classic, 4 line studies, 4 cinematic worlds
  • A crowd that dances, and raises phones in the breaks
  • Presets on Shift+1…9: four to start, nine slots of your own
  • An audience display on any screen, with blackout, strobe safety and reduced motion

02 / YOU + AI, BACK TO BACK

Your AI DJ agent gets tools, not a chat box. 🤝

The agent doesn't get a chat box in the corner. It gets tools: the same session you see, the same proposals, the same activity log. It can read structure and prepare the next move. Below the Co-DJ level, anything the crowd would hear asks you first. Every agent action has a one-tap undo.

03 / WHY WEBMCP

Why WebMCP: the agent has to be in this tab. 🪩

WebMCP lets a web page hand structured tools to a browser agent. For most apps that's a convenience. For a DJ instrument it's the only way this works, and here's why.

01

The instrument lives in the page.

The audio graph runs on an AudioWorklet, on the audio thread of this tab. A server has no address for it. The agent has to be where the decks are.

02

The timing window is milliseconds wide.

The next bar boundary exists for a few milliseconds. A network round trip would arrive after the beat. The tools return structure; the browser executes the timing, sample-accurate.

03

Your files never leave the browser.

Your tracks open through the File System Access API and are never uploaded. A cloud agent would have nothing to analyse. A page agent gets BPM, key, energy and sections — no file paths, no URLs, no audio, and track titles flagged as untrusted content.

04

You and the agent see one session.

This is visual collaboration, not remote control. The agent's proposal is a card on your console. Its call is a line in your activity log. You both look at the same waveform.

WebMCP is an experimental Community Group draft, not a finished W3C standard. Mixerx runs with it in Chrome (WebMCP enabled) and in ChatGPT's in-app browser. The manual controls and the local AI DJ agent don't need it. Chrome's WebMCP guide ↗ The draft ↗

04 / WHAT IT DOES TODAY

What the app does today. 📦

Every picture is a capture of the alpha, not a mock-up. Everything here exists today.

Two decks, real waveforms 🎚️

AudioWorklet decks with sample-accurate transport, sync, a pitch fader, nudge and a phase meter. The engine is the only source of time; nothing animates that it did not do.

A full mixer: EQ, filter, CUE 🎛️

3-band EQ with kills at 12 o'clock, filter, trims, vertical channel faders, PFL/CUE, a limiter and crossfader curves. Knobs you can grab with a MIDI controller.

It finds beats, key and sections 🧠

A worker finds the beat grid, downbeat, key (Camelot), loudness, energy per bar and the sections: intro, build, drop, break, outro. The library then tells you what fits the on-air deck: key relation, tempo delta, energy.

LEARN mode, one step at a time 🎓

Lesson 1 teaches the phrase-entry blend: exactly one control highlighted at a time, real engine state, sample-accurate ENTER, a score and a “Show me”. Badges when you finish.

PERFORM pads 🥁

Hot cues, loops, beat jump and sampler pads, all on the grid, with keyboard hints on every button and a keyboard map on ?.

A command palette for everything ⌨️

⌘K opens one search over the agent's tools and the console's own actions. The same registry the agent calls, one keystroke away for you.

Demo tracks written by the page 🧪

Press “Load demo tracks” and two original tunes are synthesised in the browser: Neon Drift (124 BPM, 8A) and Glass Tide (128 BPM, 8B). Same analysis as any file you drop in.

A Director for the stage 🎬

Scene switches land on bar boundaries, the drop flashes under a strobe budget, a camera operator cuts between six shots on the bar, and follow-sections picks the look for each section.

A crowd that dances 🕺

Classic abstract dancers or pixel characters, four to ten per scene, with their own grooves, phones up in the break and a phrase wave across the floor. Saved per scene and in presets.

Audience display + PiP 📺

Send the stage to any screen with F, black it out with Shift+B, keep a picture-in-picture preview backstage. Frames and settings travel over a channel, never audio.

Provably offline 🔒

A Content-Security-Policy with connect-src 'self', no CDN, no telemetry. On load the page fires a fetch that must fail and waits for the violation event. The badge is the proof.

Web MIDI, opt-in 🎹

Plug in a controller, enable it, and learn any knob or fader with soft takeover. Mappings are kept per controller in this browser; nothing is sent back to the device.

07 / UNDER THE HOOD

Everything runs in the browser: the audio on the audio thread, the visuals on the GPU, your library on your disk.

🔒 PRIVACY, SAID PRECISELY

Your files never leave this browser.

Not the files, not their paths, not the decoded samples, not the waveforms. Zero network requests at runtime, no analytics, no CDN. The page proves it on every load instead of promising it.

To be exact about what an agent does see: track titles and artists from your file tags, plus analysis summaries. That's what musical reasoning needs. An external AI provider handles that context under its own policies; WebMCP itself is not a privacy guarantee.

09 / BEFORE YOU HIT PLAY

Straight answers. 🙋

Early project. Big ideas. No fog machine on the facts.

Is this a finished product?+

No. Mixerx is an early alpha built around a working two-deck mixer, an agent layer and a visual Stage. Expect rough edges. The roadmap and the 3-in-1 idea are directions, not available features or launch commitments.

Do I need an AI agent to use it?+

No. The mixer and the Stage work by hand. The in-page local AI DJ agent uses deterministic rules. External agents connect through WebMCP where the browser supports it. WebMCP is the interface, not the model.

Does my music leave my computer?+

The audio doesn't. Files, paths, samples and waveforms never leave the machine, and the app makes zero network requests at runtime. Tool results contain titles, artists and analysis summaries; an external AI provider may process that context under its own policies. Check that provider's settings before connecting an agent. The optional demo on this introduction page is hosted on YouTube and connects to Google only when you press play.

Which browsers?+

Desktop Chromium (Chrome, Edge) is the reference: per-device audio output, folder pickers, window management and WebMCP live there. Firefox and Safari load the console with reduced routing and no Stage without WebGPU. The app says plainly what's missing.

Are the pictures on this page real?+

Yes. Every screenshot is a capture of the alpha in Chromium: the console with the two demo tracks the page writes itself, and the Stage running its built-in synthetic test signal. The alpha demo is a recording with sound, played only on request. This page never opens a microphone or runs a model. The 3-in-1 timeline is the one drawing, and it is labelled as a concept.

Flashes, motion, sensitivity?+

Use “Pause motion” at the top for the page animations. The demo recording contains moving visuals; use the video player's own pause control to stop it. The real Stage has its own reduced-motion, photosensitive-safe and blackout controls.

What's the license?+

AGPL-3.0-or-later, with a separate commercial license available for closed or hosted use. Fonts are OFL, the FFT ocean adapts an MIT example, and no audio is bundled with the source.
